# A fair VAE-versus-BayesWave comparison

The manuscript's "BayesWave gets 344/419 correct" claim is not a defensible
statement about BayesWave. Three separate things were wrong with it, and each is
fixed by a specific piece of this workflow.

## 1. The two pipelines were answering different questions

The VAE statistic is a Bayesian coherence ratio,

```text
ln O = ln alpha + lnZ_S - sum_i ln[ beta Z_G,i + (1 - beta) Z_N,i ]
```

(`_compute_log_bcr` in `src/starccato_lvk/analysis/main.py`, alpha = 1, beta =
0.5): signal against a **glitch-or-noise mixture**, per detector.

BayesWave's native `lnB_S/G = lnZ_S - lnZ_G` is signal against **glitch alone**.
On a quiet event both transient models are rejected and their difference is not a
detection statistic; it is the gap between two hypotheses the data has already
thrown out, and it can land anywhere.

`aligned_signal_vs_glitch_or_noise` in `src/starccato_lvk/bayeswave.py` builds the
aligned statistic from the same three evidences BayesWave already reports:

```text
lnB_S/(GvN) = ln alpha + lnZ_S - ln[ beta Z_G + (1 - beta) Z_N ]
```

with the same alpha and beta. Both statistics are now written into every
`result.json`; the native one stays because it is BayesWave's own published
quantity and dropping it would hide the difference the alignment makes.

Aligning the hypothesis is worth about +0.02 AUC to BayesWave (0.676 -> 0.698)
and cuts its false-alarm rate at the zero threshold roughly in half (0.128 ->
0.073). Real, but not the main story.

## 2. "Correct sign at zero" is not a comparison

Zero is only a shared threshold if both statistics share a normalisation. They do
not. With beta = 0.5 the VAE's denominator is a product over two detectors, so it
carries a +2 ln 2 offset; BayesWave's glitch and noise models are network-level,
so its aligned statistic carries +ln 2. The two zeros sit ln 2 apart before any
physics enters, and the structural difference (a product of per-detector
single-detector glitch evidences versus one joint incoherent glitch model) is
larger than that.

A constant offset changes every sign-agreement count and changes no ranking at
all. So the primary numbers are now:

- **AUC**, with a percentile-bootstrap 95% interval;
- **detection efficiency at a matched empirical false-positive fraction**, where
  the threshold is read off the observed real-blip background at FPF = 0.10,
  0.05, 0.01, so both pipelines are always quoted at the same measured false
  alarm rate.

`studies/bw_vae_roc.py` produces these. The old sign-at-zero number is still
reported per statistic, alongside the false-alarm rate that threshold actually
implies -- which is the point: it is an efficiency at an unmatched, uncontrolled
FPF.

## 3. The 419-event cohort was a post-hoc uncertainty cut that flattered BayesWave

The 419/344 cohort is what survives `bw_lnbf_signal_glitch_err < ~4.8` on top of
the full-quality rows. That cut is a selection on BayesWave's own reported
sampling error, and it is not neutral:

| cohort | N | sign-at-zero correct | fraction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| uncertainty cut (`err < 4.8`) | 421 | 344 | **0.817** |
| all paired rows, no cut | 1366 | 932 | **0.682** |

Keeping only the events BayesWave was most confident about raised its apparent
accuracy by 13.5 points. Reporting that number without the cut is what made the
comparison look strange next to the VAE.

The replacement cohort accounting is generated by `studies/bw_vae_roc.py` and
applies **no** uncertainty threshold. Outcomes are explicit categories:

| class | complete | salvaged evidence | BayesWave no score | VAE no score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `inj_ccsn` | 599 | 77 | 1 | 0 |
| `real_glitch` | 643 | 46 | 0 | 0 |

Salvaged evidences (reconstructed from `bayeswave.log`, or from an `evidence.dat`
whose uncertainty underflowed) stay in the primary cohort and are flagged.
Excluding them would be the same post-hoc selection in a different coat. Events
with no usable score are counted, and the report also quantifies the worst case
in which every one of them is ranked at the bottom for whichever pipeline failed.

## Current numbers (pre-pilot, 1e6 iterations)

676 injected CCSN against 689 real blips:

| statistic | AUC [95%] | eff @ FPF 0.10 | @ 0.05 | @ 0.01 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VAE `lnO_S/(G+N)` | 0.994 [0.990, 0.998] | 0.990 | 0.990 | 0.987 |
| BayesWave `lnB_S/G` (native) | 0.676 [0.647, 0.704] | 0.481 | 0.453 | 0.009 |
| BayesWave `lnB_S/(GvN)` (aligned) | 0.698 [0.672, 0.727] | 0.497 | 0.464 | 0.021 |

These are **provisional** until the convergence pilot below reports. They are
computed at BayesWave's production 1e6-iteration settings, and the entire purpose
of the pilot is to test whether those settings placed the scores correctly.

## 4. The PSD was estimated from the segment being ranked

The v0.44 campaign passed `--psdlength 4.0` against `--seglen 4.0`, so BayesWave
estimated its PSD from *the same four seconds it was analysing*:

```text
Estimating PSD for H1 using 1 segments of 8192 samples (4.000000s)
```

One periodogram realisation, two degrees of freedom per bin. BayesLine's
spline-plus-Lorentzian posterior is then almost unconstrained, and the three-seed
pilot shows what that costs:

| quantity | median seed-to-seed range |
| --- | --- |
| `lnZ_noise` | 111.7 nats |
| `lnZ_signal` | 73.1 nats |
| `lnZ_glitch` | 91.9 nats |
| `lnB_S/G` | 18.3 nats |

The noise model contains **no wavelets at all** -- it is pure BayesLine -- so a
112-nat seed dependence there is not a transient-sampling problem. All three
evidences ride the same wandering noise floor; the Bayes factor is smaller only
because part of it cancels in the difference, and it does not cancel fully
because each model runs its own independent BayesLine chain. This, not chain
length, is why 4e6 iterations did not converge.

The fix needed no new data. Every bundle already carries `full_strain`: 69 s laid
out as 64 s of off-source data, a 0.5 s gap, then the 4 s analysis segment --
exactly the manuscript's PSD recipe. Only `strain/values` was ever written to the
GWF, so BayesWave never saw the other 65 seconds. `prepare_frames` now writes
`full_strain` and sets `--psdstart`/`--psdlength` to that off-source window, so
BayesWave uses 16 four-second FFTs from **the same window length and data stretch
the VAE uses**. The downstream PSD models remain method-specific. A bundle
without `full_strain` is now
rejected unless `--allow-onsource-psd` is passed explicitly.

Every `result.json` records the PSD configuration it was produced under, so a
pre-fix evidence can never be silently pooled with a post-fix one.

## 5. Archived PSDs were stored as float32 and destroyed

`_load_dat` cast every BayesWave table to float32. A PSD in physical strain units
sits near 1e-46, below float32's smallest subnormal (1.4e-45), so the cast
flushed it to zero; the reciprocal whitening column overflowed to `inf` at the
other end. **All 1,682 stored PSDs in the v0.44 archive are damaged** -- 264
entirely zero, 1,418 partly, none clean -- which is why no usable
common-whitening pair exists for the waveform overlay.

Tables are now parsed in float64 and narrowed to float32 only where that cannot
underflow or overflow, decided per array rather than per dataset name. A rebuild
repairs a damaged dataset in place when the source `.dat` still exists (3,364 do)
and leaves it alone when it does not, so the repair can never replace real data
with nothing.

This blocks only the waveform-level overlay. The sign comparison needs
`evidence.dat` alone and is unaffected.

## The pre-registered trust check

The old 419-event table is retired. It was selected using BayesWave's reported
uncertainty, while the first three-seed pilot showed that this uncertainty could
substantially understate run-to-run scatter under the broken on-source PSD setup.
The replacement is intentionally small and makes only a sign-level trust claim.

`studies/select_bw_pilot_events.py` selects the first 12 numerical indices from
each prepared H1--L1 manifest population: 12 injected CCSNe and 12 cataloged
blips. It reads no BayesWave result, score, uncertainty, or agreement label.
This produces 24 attempted events and 72 runs under seeds 11, 22, and 33.

`slurm/bayeswave_seed_pilot.sh` runs the cohort at 4e6 iterations. Both methods
use the same strain, trigger, 300--800 Hz band, fixed sky, and underlying 64 s
off-source PSD window. BayesWave still uses its own wavelet and PSD models, so
the two numerical statistics are not treated as commensurate.

`studies/bw_pilot_convergence.py` reports all three evidences and both
`lnB_S/G` and `lnB_S/(G or N)`. An event is seed-consistent only if all requested
seeds complete with finite S/G/N evidences and all seeds agree on the non-zero
sign of both derived statistics. Reported evidence uncertainty is retained as a
diagnostic but never used to select events or decide acceptance. The output
includes every attempted event, numerical seed ranges, convergence-failure
counts, and the manuscript-ready four-cell contingency table on the
seed-consistent subset.

## How to state the result

The comparison is a **CCSN-targeted VAE against a morphology-agnostic BayesWave
check**. BayesWave is given no CCSN waveform family, training set, or targeted
morphological prior. Agreement in sign supports the learned-prior result;
disagreement is not evidence against the VAE and must be interpreted in light of
the harder question BayesWave is being asked.

## Running it

The pilot reads its inputs from oz303 but **writes to oz980**. Each BayesWave run
holds about 600 files while sampling, so 72 runs at 24-way concurrency need
about 15k spare inodes; oz303 was at 97% of its 1M-file group quota when this pilot
was launched, and exhausting it kills every job in the group at startup with
`RaisedSignal:53`, not just this one. Check `lfs quota -g oz303 /fred` before
moving it back.

```bash
R=/fred/oz303/avajpeyi/results/starccato_lvk
P=/fred/oz980/avajpeyi/results/starccato_lvk/bw_seed_pilot

python studies/select_bw_pilot_events.py \
  --ccsn-manifests $R/bwcomp_nml_v044_ccsn/data/rn_H1_L1 \
  --glitch-manifests $R/bwcomp_nml_v044_glitch/data/rn_H1_L1 \
  --out $P/pilot_cohort.json

sbatch --array=0-71%24 slurm/bayeswave_seed_pilot.sh

python studies/bw_pilot_convergence.py \
  --pilot-root $P \
  --paired $R/paired_lno_vs_bayeswave.csv \
  --table ../manuscript/bayeswave_sign_table.tex

python studies/bw_vae_roc.py \
  --paired $R/paired_lno_vs_bayeswave.csv \
  --outdir $R/comparison_v2
```
